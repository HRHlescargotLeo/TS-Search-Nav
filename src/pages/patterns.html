How other firms do it

How ten UK and international law firms organise their main menus, set against the questions still open after Workshop 1. Checked on 20 October 2026.

What it means for the models

  1. Every firm we could read keeps People in the main menu. Nine of nine; Linklaters goes further and puts ‘Find a Lawyer’ first. Removing the People menu, as Models C and D do, would make Travers Smith the exception. Keeping it does not rule out simplifying what is inside it.
  2. The usual pattern is two parallel lists: practices and sectors. Slaughter and May, Burges Salmon, Osborne Clarke, Simmons & Simmons and Linklaters all do a version of it. Model A is closest to this.
  3. ‘A few focus areas, then everything’ is the second pattern. Macfarlanes leads with four core areas and an ‘All services’ link; Simmons & Simmons with five core sectors; Fieldfisher with featured services and an A–Z page. This is Model D’s Services menu.
  4. None of the firms we could read leads its main menu with client issues. Where client or issue framing appears, it is in the insights menu: Macfarlanes’ ‘Insights for your role’, ‘Key topics’ at Osborne Clarke and HSF Kramer, ‘Featured Topics’ at Linklaters. Model C would be distinctive. That is an opportunity and a risk, and it depends on the issue pages being written.
  5. Nobody puts sub-services in the menu. The longest full list we found is Slaughter and May’s 28 practices. Travers Smith has 31 practices and 82 sub-services; Model A shows the sub-services only behind expand buttons and the filter box.
  6. Overview text inside a menu panel is rare; promoted content is common. Only Burges Salmon’s About us panel had an introduction. Macfarlanes, Burges Salmon and Osborne Clarke all promote a case study, article or vacancy inside their panels. In the prototype that is the panel-top option: a short overview (Models B and C) or a promo banner (Model D).

On search engines

Niall asked what is lost if not every service is in the menu. For search engines, a service page needs to be reachable through an ordinary link, not necessarily a menu link. In every model the A–Z of services is linked from the menu and the footer on every page, so every service page stays linked. What a shorter menu gives up is mainly the one-click route for visitors, which the filter box and search are there to cover. This is general guidance, not a ranking guarantee.
